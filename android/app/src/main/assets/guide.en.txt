Stroik Android — guide

Accessibility
Bottom buttons open Tuner, Instrument, Settings and Help. Use TalkBack gestures to navigate standard buttons, checkboxes, dropdowns and labelled fields. Controls have large touch targets. Android announces checkbox state. Only final readings are announced, not every audio sample. Without TalkBack, system speech may read results. Graphics are optional and off by default. Polish/English and dark theme are in Settings.

Methods
Tuning fork plays a decaying reference. Enable automatic strikes and set an interval or use Strike tuning fork. Pause/resume controls automatic playback.
Electronic tuner requires microphone permission. Start microphone, play one note and mute it. The result gives note, direction and deviation. Stop microphone ends listening. Leaving the app stops capture and playback; start again after returning. Audio is not saved or uploaded.
Speaker hybrid plays a 3-second reference, ignores capture for 3.4 seconds, listens to your instrument, announces the reading, waits about 5 seconds and repeats. Long screen reader speech or room reverberation may require stopping and retrying.
Headphone hybrid listens while the reference plays. Optional microphone monitoring requires connected headphones. Do not monitor through speakers: feedback can occur. Latency depends on hardware. Default Android devices are used without changing system settings.

Instruments and notes
Piano and chromatic modes cover 88 notes, A0 to C8, and always show the octave dropdown. Default target: one-line G (G4). Guitar, ukulele, violin and bass presets list open strings. Ukulele standard, floor upward: A E C G; alternative G E C A. Guitar, thickest first: E A D G B E. Use chromatic mode for other tunings. Piano order can ascend, alternate blocks of five semitones to either side, or alternate whole-tone blocks. Next note advances manually.
In electronic mode, two strikes after an in-tune result can advance. Fork and hybrid modes have a separate two-attack gesture; incidental noises can also trigger it. Set the gap from 0.5 to 2.5 seconds. The app's speaker reference is ignored, and automatic fork strikes with the gesture enabled are at least 6.5 seconds apart.

Consonance
Select the lower target, then Fifths or Octaves. C4 pairs with G4 or C5. Fork mode plays the pair together. Separate measurement: lower, mute, confirmation, upper, mute. Results give deviation from 700/1200 cents and estimated ideal-harmonic beats: |2×upper−3×lower| or |upper−2×lower| Hz.
Together mode is experimental: play near the selected target for about 2 seconds. A lower note's harmonic may mimic a second note, especially an octave. Confirm separately. This cannot certify no audible beating. Equal-tempered fifths can naturally beat, and piano inharmonicity can require stretched octaves.

Reliable readings
Strikes that are too weak or too strong can cause inaccurate readings. Play evenly and avoid clipping. Change sensitivity in Instrument. Manual correction ±100 cents requires comparison with a known reference. Displaying tenths of a Hz does not guarantee that accuracy. A=440 Hz is standard. Have old pianos assessed before raising tension; age alone does not determine safe pitch. Practise aural tuning on a modest instrument first; broken strings can still injure.

Profiles and updates
Save/Open settings file use Android's document picker for JSON profiles. Profiles include method, instrument, language, theme and update settings. Loading stops the current measurement. Automatic checks run on entry and at the selected interval while the app is open. They can be disabled. No-update and network failure checks are silent in the background. Manual checking is in Settings.
A new release shows notes and asks to download. SHA-256, package name, increasing version code and signing certificate are checked. Android then asks for installation confirmation, possibly after allowing installations from Stroik. Updates are not installed silently. Successful updates preserve settings.
